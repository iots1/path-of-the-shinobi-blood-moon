- **Right-hand finger rig (2026-10-19) — `assets/ninja.glb` now has 47 joints (28 + 19 new + the old `RightHandMiddle4` moved), RIGHT hand only; superseded 2026-10-20 by the left-hand pass below, which brought both hands to 66 joints total.** Added in Blender (`blender-ninja/new_ninja_fingers_right.blend`; scripts in `blender-ninja/finger_rig_scripts/` — they have scratchpad paths hardcoded at the top, adjust before re-running): `mixamorig:RightHand{Index,Middle,Ring,Pinky,Thumb}{1,2,3,4}` (1–3 deform, 4 = tip nub), names exactly what `prepFingers()` looks up, so the game picks them up with no code change (`real.fingers` = 5 entries, `setFingers()` now actually curls the right hand).   - **How the joints were found:** this Meshy hand is a mesh with five separate prongs that droop 35–50° off the hand bone's axis, so a straight-axis analysis failed (k-means in a hand-axis frame, connected-components cut by depth: both merged fingers). What worked: geodesic distance along the mesh from the wrist → fingertips = local maxima (6, two of them 5 mm apart = one fingertip) → each vertex flows uphill to a tip → per-finger centerline from the centroid of geodesic-distance bins → joints at 28/60/100 % of the finger length from the tip (index/middle/ring .070 m, pinky .055, thumb .075 — read from where the cross-section grows into the palm). Phalanges come out 1.5–3 cm.
  - **Weights:** for every vertex with RightHand weight >0.01, nearest finger polyline (MCP→PIP→DIP→tip→nub); within 1.25 cm = full finger, falling to none at 1.85 cm; ramp from the Hand bone to the finger chain over −0.4…+1.2 cm past the MCP; smoothstep blends around PIP/DIP; **max 4 influences per vertex** (three.js ignores more; the exporter also truncates). Bone roll aligned to the Hand bone's +Z (thumb side). Checked by posing in Blender (`FIST.curl` ×1 and ×0.55 about each bone's local Z) — a clean fist, no spikes from the palm — and in-game (contact sheet through the 3-beat combo, close zoom at idle: fingers wrap the hilt).
  - **Export gotchas hit (all Blender 5.2):** (1) the glb's clips are **30 fps** (20 / 32 samples = 0.6333 / 1.0333 s) but Blender imports at 24 fps, leaving keys at fractional frames (15.2 / 24.8) — a plain re-export samples integer frames and silently shortens both clips (1.00 / 0.62 s), which the game's `calibrateClip` would then re-measure differently. (3) The exported clips now carry loc/rot/scale channels for all 47 bones (141 channels, the non-animated ones with 2 constant samples) instead of 56 — harmless, the game only reads the 8 leg quaternion tracks. - **Per-finger/per-side grip-radius tuning (2026-10-20) — `FIST.wrapCurl`/`thumbPd`/`thumbScale`, both hands.** The user asked specifically whether the sword sits snugly between thumb and index with all 5 fingers wrapped to the blade's actual radius — closer inspection (close-zoom combo screenshots from a yaw that doesn't hide one hand behind the other, cross-checked against direct fingertip-position measurements in the sword's own local space) showed it didn't: the single shared curl amount per hand (`FIST.gripR` for the right hand, the twoEff-ramped `kL` for the left) put the 4 fingertips at very different radii from the hilt axis for the SAME curl scale — e.g. the right hand at `gripR=.65` measured 1.41–3.09 cm against the tsuka's own ≈1.46–1.80 cm oval radius (`tsukaGeo` in the sword prop, section 1) — because each finger's fingertip traces a DIFFERENT radius-vs-curl-scale curve (shorter/outer fingers need noticeably more curl to close the same distance as the index), and the two hands differ further since `GRIP_CFG.R.phi` (75°) vs `.L.phi` (30°) rotate the whole hand very differently around the hilt.
  - **Fix:** `FIST.wrapCurl = {R:{...}, L:{...}}`, a per-side/per-finger scale multiplied into the existing curl amount (`ang[i]*k*wrap` in `setFingers`), each value found by driving that one finger's 3 joints directly (bypassing the render loop) and scanning/bisecting the scale for where the fingertip lands at that finger's own angle-dependent hilt-surface radius + ~0.3 cm margin (so the fingertip MESH's own thickness reads as touching the surface, not sunk in or floating off — a pure point-target undershoots the visual contact). Values: R `{Index:.41, Middle:.56, Ring:.71, Pinky:.78}`, L `{Index:.69, Middle:.71, Ring:.84, Pinky:.87}` — a fairly smooth index→pinky gradient on each side, which is what made the per-finger (not per-hand) table credible rather than overfit noise.
  - **The thumb needed its curl AXIS re-aimed, not just scaled.** Scanning the old shared `thumbPd` (with the old `-sgn`-mirror trick) over a wide curl-scale range only ever swept the right thumb from −167° to −124° around the hilt (measuring each fingertip's angle in the sword's own local XZ plane); the target — opposite the circular mean of the 4 (now-corrected) finger angles — is ≈+150°. Reaching that by scale alone would need `scale≈3.5` (a grotesquely over-curled, almost certainly self-intersecting thumb), not a real fix. Instead did a Fibonacci-sphere search over candidate `thumbPd` DIRECTIONS (not just magnitude) × a sane curl-scale range (0.2–1.2), minimizing angular distance to the opposite-of-fingers target plus radius error — found per-side directions that reach the target at a normal-order scale (`thumbScale.R=1`, `.L=.25` — the left thumb needed much less curl at its new axis to land at the same radius, same kind of hand-to-hand asymmetry as `GRIP_CFG`'s `phi`). `FIST.thumbPd` is now `{R:[...], L:[...]}` (two independent vectors) instead of one shared vector mirrored by `-sgn` — `prepFingers()` no longer flips it per side.
  - **Verified:** direct fingertip measurements after the fix land within ≈0.02–0.25 cm of the hilt-radius target and within ≈1–4° of the thumb's opposite-of-fingers target, both hands, at an actual `S.twoEff===1` combo frame.
