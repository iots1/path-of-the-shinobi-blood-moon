### Current model (2026-10-18 swap — read this first for everything else below; superseded by the no-hood swap above for the character asset itself)
Source: `blender-ninja/new_ninja_original/Meshy_AI_Ninja_Remeshed_for_Ri_All_Animations.glb` (remeshed 10k-tri body, one 4096² base-colour + 4096² metallic/roughness texture, no normal map) copied unchanged to `assets/ninja.glb`. Nothing was edited in Blender.
- **Bones are Mixamo-named** (`mixamorig:Hips`, `…Spine/Spine1/Spine2`, `…Neck`, `…LeftHandIndex1`…). three.js strips the colon (`mixamorigHips`). The loader's `canonBone()` maps them back to the OLD short names so the rest of the code is unchanged: Mixamo `Spine`→`Spine02`, `Spine1`→`Spine01`, `Spine2`→`Spine`, `Neck`→`neck`, `HeadTop_End`→`head_end`; everything else just loses the prefix (`LeftArm`, `LeftHandThumb1`, …). `real.bone` / `real.bind` are keyed by those names. Track names of the clips go through `canonBone` too.
- **Units are metres, no 0.01 Armature scale** (the old model was in cm under a 0.01 scale). Anything that was "cm" is now metres: eye positions, `BACK_SWORDS`. Faces +Z, right hand on −X: no flip needed.
- **Walk/run: the old clips can NOT be reused** (different bone names and different bind rotations — the clips store local rotations relative to the old bind). The new glb ships its own `Walking` (1.04 s) / `Running` (0.67 s) clips from the same rig, so `loadLocoClips(real, gltf.animations)` reads them from `ninja.glb`; `assets/anim/` no longer exists (the old clips are in the backup folder). Calibration/blending code is unchanged.
- **Arms are shorter and the rig's elbow is close to the shoulder**: upper arm .125–.132 m, forearm .257 m, reach ≈.389 m (old: .456). Hand targets were authored for .456, so `real.armK` (= reach/`ARM_REF`) pulls the FREE off-hand targets toward the mid-shoulder point (`toReach()` in the pose function); the sword-hand/dual keys use `real.armKSword` = 1 (authored reach; the reach clamp handles the rest). Scaling the sword-hand keys too (tried, about the right shoulder and about the mid-shoulder) broke the two-hand grip (left hand out of reach) and made beat 2 miss the dummy; the free hand needed it (elbow stayed at ~160° otherwise). Re-check `free-arm elbow bend` + two-hand grip if you change either.
- **Beat 1 (一) hand targets rescaled to this rig's reach (2026-10-19).** Every key's (hx,hy,hz) was authored for the .456 m reach of the PREVIOUS model; this rig only reaches .388 m. Since every key overreached (by 1.7–9.6 cm), `gripPass`'s reach-clamp pinned the wrist at max extension on every single key — and because a 2-bone IK's elbow angle is a pure function of wrist distance, clamping every key to the same max distance gave the identical "locked straight" elbow (measured: 161°, unchanged for the whole 260–440 ms strike + follow-through) regardless of which key was driving it. Read as a stiff, robotic swing, and crowded both hands into the same spot on the hilt (reach-clamped "G follows the hand" leaves no room between the two hands' grip points) — the likely cause of "จับดาบไม่ชัด" too. Fix: rescaled each key's (hx,hy,hz) toward the shoulder to land at ~.35–.37 m (still inside reach, not jammed against it), `lunge` raised 2.6→2.9 to recover some of the lost forward reach. Result: elbow now swings 74°–161° through the cut (was flat 161° throughout), the off-hand sits visibly lower on the grip instead of overlapping the right hand, hits still land on all 3 beats, grip ≥0.85 except the pre-existing first-run-after-reload flake.
- **Beats 2/3, K, L rescaled the same way but more conservatively (2026-10-19) — do not apply beat 1's "pull in every key" approach elsewhere without re-testing.** Pulling in beat 2's HIT key (210ms) moved the dummy from along the blade's edge to right at the grip (hitTest's `u` — position along the blade segment — collapsed to ~0; the normal-beat test requires `u>.25`, so this is a deterministic miss, not the old reload flake). Worse: pulling in beat 2's 150ms key ALSO broke the hit even with the hit key itself left untouched — the elbow-swivel search is history-dependent (picks the branch closest to last frame's elbow; see "Elbow / arm naturalness pass" above), so changing an EARLIER key can walk the solve onto a different branch that is still active when the hit key arrives, moving the actual (smoothed) grip even though its authored target never moved. Found this only by checking each key change in isolation with `m.run()`×6–8 after every edit. The policy that survived testing: **leave the HIT key and any key immediately before it untouched**, and pull in only keys that are clearly not adjacent to the hit:
  - Beat 2 (二, hit=210ms): only 70ms pulled in (.51→.384 m); 150ms/210ms/360ms stay original. `lunge`/`hitPad` stay original (hit geometry didn't move).
  - Beat 3 (三, hit=420ms): 330ms and 640ms pulled in (~.45→.384 m); the hit key (420ms, 9 cm over — the worst in the move) is left alone, same reasoning as ATTACK2/ISSEN below. `lunge` unchanged.
  - K / ATTACK2 (hit=260ms): wind-up (170ms) was already in reach; only the hold (400ms, not the hit key) pulled in. The hit key itself (5.3 cm over) is left extended on purpose — a thrust's whole point is a fully extended arm at the stab, so the lock reads as correct, not stiff.
  - K K / DUAL: barely overreaches (2 of 10 keys, ≤2.5 cm) — checked visually (bent elbows throughout, hands clearly separated on both hilts), left untouched.
  - L / ISSEN (hit=640ms): the hit key overreaches the most of any move (9.4 cm) but is a held, charge-and-release finisher — the brief extension during the hold reads as tension, not stiffness, so it's left alone along with 575ms (right before it, same adjacency risk as beat 2). Only 980ms (after the hit, lowest risk) is pulled in.
  - **Beat 2 stiffness: investigated further (2026-10-19), not fixable by repositioning keys — documented so this isn't re-attempted the same way.** The user asked for more elbow bend in beat 2 specifically; the fix above (only the 70ms key pulled in) turned out to give ZERO visible change — confirmed by holding a static pose at each candidate (`S.act` set to a fake one-key def, run ~150 frames to let the 5 Hz SOD fully converge, then read the REAL `Hand.getWorldPosition` vs shoulder distance) and found the actual constraint isn't G's distance at all: `gripPass` clamps the WRIST (`G` minus the palm offset, `grip.pt` rotated by the blade quaternion — about 8.6 cm long), and that offset's effect on distance-from-shoulder depends on the key's OWN blade angle (`by`/`el`), not a fixed amount. For the 150ms key's angles, the offset pointed such that `G` had to be scaled to ≤~80% of the way to the shoulder before the WRIST actually came inside the .388 m reach (at 90% it was still clamped) — but the hit test (measured with `m.run()`-equivalent presses) only survives scales ≥90%. The two requirements (un-clamp the wrist vs. keep the hit) don't overlap at all for this key — not a narrow margin to tune, a hard non-overlap. Also ruled out: retiming the 150ms key earlier/later (71–200ms, hit stays at 210) changes nothing — the break isn't a convergence-time problem, it's that an unclamped wrist frees the elbow-swivel solver to pick a genuinely different branch (continuity-biased, so it then STAYS on that branch through the hit key regardless of how much time passes, per the "Elbow / arm naturalness pass" hysteresis). Conclusion: fixing this would mean controlling the swivel branch directly for this move (a global `GRIP_TUNE`, not currently per-move) or giving the whole move more time/retiming the hit (changes the combo's rhythm/feel, needs the user's call) — not a hand-target tweak. Left as shipped (70ms key only); beat 2's arm stays close to extended through most of the cut, same as a fast, committed flick rather than a wind-up-and-swing — reasonable for a quick rising cut, but not what was asked for, and no further attempt should re-try "just pull in another key" without reading this first.
  - Verified after each move: `m.run()` ×6–10 (10/10 "hits on the dummy" + finisher/dual checks pass; the only recurring flake is the pre-existing "combo (standing) two-hand grip" first-attack-after-reload issue, 4/10 of this session's runs, values 0.00–0.19 — matches the documented behavior, not a new regression) plus `m.sheet()` contact sheets from the front, side and back for each move.
  - **Post-attack idle "droop" fixed (2026-10-19); fixing it properly unmasked the grip-timing flake onto EVERY attack, accepted as a known issue — read before touching `swivel()`'s hold, `S.rho`, or `gripOn` again.** The user reported the right arm settling into a visibly unbalanced idle pose after any J combo (measured: post-combo wrist position ~5 cm off and blade-roll `S.rho.R`≈1.0 vs. a fresh page load's 0, unchanged even after 10+ s of idle) and asked that every pose return to the same values as a fresh reload. Root cause: the sword-hand's elbow-swivel search (`gripPass`→`swivel()`) and the blade-roll angle (`S.rho.R`, `rollToward`) feed each other — `swivel()`'s cost includes `swivelBias*|al|`, a permanent pull toward angle 0 (the pole-based "natural" elbow), but on an actual fresh load `arm.eRel` is also falsy so `swivel()`'s continuity term degenerates to an EXTRA `hold*|al|` pull toward 0 too (total ≈0.65); after an attack `arm.eRel` is non-null (stale), so that extra pull targets the STALE elbow instead, and `roR` (rho's own target) is computed FROM that same stale elbow — the two settle into a second, self-consistent but non-natural fixed point that never drifts back, no matter how long the idle. First fix tried: force `arm.eRel=null` at the idle handoff to mimic a fresh load — wrong, null `eRel` also disables `swivel()`'s rate cap (meant for a true first frame, which has nothing to ease from); mid-game this is a visible snap, measured wrist peak 25,000+°/s vs. the 12,000 limit. Reverted. Second tried: ramp `swivel()`'s `hold` down to ~0 for a brief window post-idle — wrong reasoning (lowering hold REMOVES the pull-to-natural instead of restoring it, since a fresh load's pull comes from `hold` being FULL with `prev=0`, not from `hold` being low); measured no improvement at all. **Actual fix**: `swivelBiasNow()`/`roRNow`/`roLNow` (section 3b, `IDLE_RELAX_MS`, right after `liftCost`) keep `hold` at its normal full value but bias the search toward angle 0 (and ease `S.rho.R`/`S.rho.L` toward 0) explicitly for 500 ms after an attack ends — not toward the stale `prev`/`roR` — while the actual easing/rate-cap (`tauSwivel`, `swivelRate`, `tauRoll`, `rollRate`) still run off the real, unchanged state, so the correction is smooth, not a pop. `startAttack()` resets `S.idleRelax=0` so a leftover relax window from the PREVIOUS idle period can never bias an ACTIVE attack's swivel/roll (first version of this fix didn't do that and broke the two-hand grip in every combo, not just the first one — see below). Verified: a fresh-load vs. post-3-beat-combo-then-2.5s-idle measurement now matches to within noise (wristPos within 0.2 mm, `S.rho.R` 0 vs. 0.001, elbow angle identical) where it previously differed by ~5 cm / ~1.0 rad.
  - **Side effect, accepted as a known issue (2026-10-19): the off-hand grip now flickers in EVERY attack that starts from idle, not just the page's first one.** Before this fix, `arm.eRel`/`S.rho` never actually returned to the true fresh-load values after an attack (that was the droop bug), which happened to also keep the off-hand's grip search away from a narrow bend/cross-body threshold that a genuine fresh start sits right on top of. Fixing the droop means every idle→attack transition now reproduces the exact fresh-load starting condition — including this pre-existing, previously-"1 of 5, first attack only" flake, which is now deterministic on every attack through beats 1 and 3 (confirmed on the UNMODIFIED pre-droop-fix code too, with `S.rho` forced to 0 before a fresh-reload press: same bend/crossL numbers, same failure — this is not something the droop fix introduced, only something it stopped accidentally masking). Mechanism (beat 1, 一): the off-hand's `gripOn` hysteresis (section 3b, `const lw=...,crossL=...`) releases when the best reachable slide position along the hilt (`gripAt(sd)`, searched over `sd∈[-.06,-.17]`, the documented valid "6–17 cm below the right hand" range) needs a wrist bend >140° or crosses the body past `crossL<-.14`; during beat 1's 190→310 ms wind-up-to-impact sweep (the overhead key1 at `el:128` unwinding fast into the impact key), bend and crossL both cross their thresholds around 270–312 ms, and a similar crossing recurs mid-beat-3. Tried and rejected: (1) widening the slide-search range toward the right hand (`sd`→0/positive) or further past the pommel (`sd`→−0.30) to find a technically-sub-140°/sub-threshold spot — this DOES exist geometrically, but only outside the documented-valid hilt range (overlapping the right hand's own grip point, or sliding off the end of the tsuka), so using it would make `gripOn` report "fine" while the actually-rendered hand (still confined to the valid range) sits at a >140° wrist bend — a worse, hidden version of the same problem, not a fix. (2) Softening beat 1's key1 `el` from 128→95 (a non-hit, non-adjacent-to-hit key, per the established reach-fix policy) — measured ZERO improvement (`m.run()` combo-standing min-grip 0.0012 vs. 0.0007 before). This is the same class of problem as the beat-2 stiffness dead end above (`gripPass`'s wrist-reach clamp vs. the hit test "don't overlap at all for this key") — the choreography's own speed/shape through this transition and the off-hand's physically-valid hilt range are in genuine conflict, not a narrow margin. A real fix would mean re-timing/re-shaping beat 1 and 3's wind-up-to-impact swing (changes their rhythm/feel) or a global swivel-branch control, same as beat 2 — asked the user, who chose to keep the droop fix and accept this as a known limitation rather than re-choreograph. Visually mostly unnoticeable: `m.sheet({mode:'combo'})`'s per-frame `2h` (twoEff) label reads 1.0 at every sampled frame except the deliberate grab/release at the very start/end — the dip is a brief (~20–40 ms) sub-sampling-interval blip, not an obviously broken pose. `motion_check.js`'s "combo (standing)", "combo (from a run)" and "finisher L" two-hand-grip checks will now reliably FAIL (not flake) on `m.run()`; this is expected and documented here, not a regression to chase.
- **Character sometimes rendered flat white instead of its ink-brush grey (2026-10-19), fixed — GLTFLoader's own texture loading, not an asset problem.** The user saw the whole character (body, cloak, hood) as plain white with no surface detail, in-game, after a plain reload. Root cause: this model's base-colour texture is a large embedded PNG (4096², ~16 MB), and three.js's `GLTFLoader` intermittently resolves its `onLoad`/`parse` callback with the body's `MeshToonMaterial.map` left `null` — no error thrown or logged either way, so it was silent. Confirmed this is a genuine upstream race, not a corrupt asset or anything from the shoulder-mesh fix: (1) the exact same PNG bytes, extracted from the glb and decoded directly via `createImageBitmap`/`Image()`, succeeded every single time across dozens of attempts, in isolation; (2) repeated `new GLTFLoader().load()`/`.parse()` calls on the identical file, back to back in the same page, sometimes attached the texture and sometimes didn't, with no pattern tied to which glb (current or the pre-shoulder-fix backup) or how many prior loads happened; (3) a genuine fresh page reload reproduced the white character on some reloads and not others. Fix (`embeddedImageTexture()`, right before `loadNinja()`): `loadNinja()` now fetches `assets/ninja.glb` itself, hands that one `ArrayBuffer` to `GLTFLoader.parse()` for the skeleton/mesh/animations as before, and ALSO decodes the base-colour image (glb image index 0) directly from the same buffer (manually walking the GLB's JSON+BIN chunk layout, `createImageBitmap` on the extracted PNG bytes) — bypassing GLTFLoader's texture pipeline for this one assignment entirely, since that direct path never failed in testing. `skinned.material.map` is now always this independently-decoded texture, never whatever GLTFLoader's own resolution produced. `normalMap` is left as GLTFLoader's own (this model has none, so it's always `null` — harmless either way). If this model's texture is ever swapped for a much smaller one, this workaround is probably no longer needed, but it's harmless to leave in.
  - **First version of this fix (`imageOrientation:'flipY'` in the `createImageBitmap` call) looked fine at the default camera but turned the whole character into a "shattered glass" mess of random dark/grey/skin-tone patches at any close zoom — a second, worse bug, not the white-character one coming back.** Diagnosed by swapping the live material's `map` between GLTFLoader's own (working) texture and mine: GLTFLoader's looked clean at close zoom, mine didn't, even though a flat canvas preview of my decoded bitmap looked fine at a glance. Pinned it down by cropping the exact same 300×300 region of both bitmaps at 1:1 — GLTFLoader's crop and a version I decoded with `imageOrientation:'none'` were pixel-for-pixel the same (sharp, matching detail); the `imageOrientation:'flipY'` crop of the SAME region was visibly degraded (fine noisy grain instead of sharp shapes) — `createImageBitmap`'s flip path silently produces lower-quality output for this large (4096²) image in this Chromium build, with no error or size/dimension change to signal it. Fix: decode with `imageOrientation:'none'` (unflipped, matching what GLTFLoader itself does) and rely on `texture.flipY=false` for the correct orientation, instead of pre-flipping the bitmap. Verified after this correction: 8/8 fresh fetch+decode attempts and 3/3 real page reloads attached the texture correctly, and a close-zoom screenshot (zoomT 0.65, the practical minimum) matches GLTFLoader's own working render exactly — no shattering, no white character. **If this texture-loading code is ever touched again, verify at a close zoom (`__iso.CAM.zoomT≈0.65`), not just the default camera distance** — the white-character bug was obvious at any zoom, but this one only showed up close up.

- **Right shoulder mesh asymmetry (2026-10-19), fixed in Blender — `new_ninja_shoulder_fix.blend`, `blender-ninja/`.** The user reported the right shoulder reading as "not balanced"/drooping compared to the left even with the outline fixed.   - **What actually works: reshape via nearest-SURFACE-point, touch ONLY position, leave weights and UV alone.** Triangulate a copy of the mesh, build a `mathutils.bvhtree.BVHTree.FromPolygons` from just the LEFT side's triangles (faces whose centroid falls in the region), then for every RIGHT-side vertex in the mirrored region: query `bvh.find_nearest((-x,y,z))`, and move the vertex to the mirror of the hit location, `(-hit.x, hit.y, hit.z)`. Leave the vertex's existing weights and UV completely untouched. Result: 1,446 vertices moved, max displacement 2.75 cm, only 3 leftover sliver faces (pre-existing, not new), non-manifold edge count unchanged (15,260) — clean under material shading and in the actual game pose, arm reads as its own limb again.
  - **Tried and rejected — nearest-VERTEX (not nearest-surface) snapping.** Built a KD-tree of LEFT vertex positions and snapped each RIGHT vertex to its single nearest LEFT vertex. Looked fine in Blender's solid-shaded screenshots, but the right side has far more vertices in this region than the left (913 vs. 558), so many right vertices collapsed onto the SAME left vertex — **257 of 738 faces in the patched region came out with zero area**, rendering in-game as the jagged dark "torn cloth" look the user flagged from a screenshot. Diagnosed by checking `f.calc_area()` on every face in the region (not caught by eye in Blender's own viewport — the degenerate triangles are only visible under the game's toon shading/outline at a steep angle). Nearest-surface-point (above) doesn't have this problem since the query point is free to land anywhere on a triangle, not just at its corners.
  - **Tried and rejected — also replacing vertex-group weights via barycentric blend of the hit triangle's 3 corners, remapped `Left↔Right` by name.** Fixed the shape correctly (confirmed by screenshots in Blender's T-pose/bind pose — looked perfect there), but broke skinning: in the actual game pose (arm rotated down from the shoulder), the re-weighted vertices visually fused into one surface with the back cloak, curving oddly instead of reading as a distinct limb. Bind-pose screenshots in Blender can't catch this — the arm and torso/cloak are both roughly in their rest positions there, so a weight error only shows once the bones actually rotate apart.
