This file keeps every detail: rigs, leg correction, ground snap, locomotion clips, arm/grip continuity, every attack (beats 1–3, Space blink, K, K K, L), torso/off-hand, hands/grip (older fist model), material, back cloak, back swords, debug tips. ### Client architecture (know this before making changes)
- Units = meters. The character faces +Z; the character's right hand is on the -X side. Height 1.75 m.
- **Day / night + character shading (2026-10-21).** `TIME_OF_DAY` / `setTimeOfDay()` (lights section): `N` key or the "☾ Night" button, persisted in `localStorage` `potsbm_time`. Night = cool dimmer hemi/sun, dark clear colour, paper overlay at .55, lantern point lights (always in the scene at intensity 0 in daytime, so toggling doesn't recompile materials). Character material: soft-edged ramp `rampTex(85,175,.38,.6,.08)` (was a hard 3-px 118/190/255 ramp — blotchy cel patches on every fold), anisotropic filtering on the 4096² maps, `sun.shadow.normalBias .02` (self-shadow speckles). **`applyBodyShading()`** (before `loadNinja`): all cloth folds / leg wraps / facial relief live in the NORMAL map (the base colour is nearly flat dark grey), and the toon ramp flattened them → body read as one grey lump. It adds view-space fold relief from a mip-biased (blurred) normal-map sample (large folds, strong) + the fine remainder (weak), crease darkening, a faint cloth rim and skin sheen, with a skin mask from the albedo (warm pixels) so skin keeps its painted tones and the dark-cloth gamma lift (`0.03+0.97*pow(c,.68)`) only hits cloth. Live-tune `__iso.rig.real.shade.uFold.value` etc.; `uFoldBias` > 2 bleeds the flat atlas background in at UV seams (block artefacts). The normal map is now decoded with `embeddedImageTexture()` too (same GLTFLoader race as the base colour). No Blender change was needed.
- **Color pipeline is pinned to r128 behavior**: `THREE.ColorManagement.enabled=false`, `renderer.outputColorSpace=LinearSRGBColorSpace`, `renderer.useLegacyLights=true` (deprecated in 0.160, removed in r165 — upgrading Three.js past that means retuning light intensities). Don't flip these without comparing screenshots.
- **Two rigs.** The procedural rig (nested `THREE.Group`s in `rig`: `root → hips → spine → chest → neck → head`, legs `thighL/R → kneeL/R → ankleL/R`) is now an **invisible driver**. `applySkeleton()` poses it exactly as before, then `driveRealBones()` copies each driver's rotation delta (relative to its rest) onto the matching real bone of `assets/ninja.glb` (`BONE_MAP`, section `3b`). If the glb fails to load, the procedural body stays visible as a fallback.
  - This model's bones have **no naming prefix** (`Hips`, `Spine`, `LeftArm`, …) and the spine order is reversed vs. a typical Mixamo rig — `Spine02` is closest to the hips, `Spine` (no suffix) is closest to the neck. See `docs/BONES.md`.
  - The Armature node carries a **0.01 scale** (mesh authored in cm-like units). Two consequences: (1) quaternions must be extracted via `Matrix4.extractRotation()` first, never `Quaternion.setFromRotationMatrix()` on a matrix that still has this scale baked in — it silently produces a non-unit quaternion and the whole rig collapses; (2) a `Mesh` parented directly as a child of one of these `Bone` objects doesn't render (looks like a frustum-culling quirk) — world-space props must be positioned every frame via `matrixWorld`, never literally `bone.add(prop)`.
  - Meshy's bind pose has the legs splayed; `real.corr` straightens the legs first so `spread` etc. mean the same as on the driver. **Important:** correcting each leg segment independently (thigh and knee each rotated to point straight down on their own) leaves the skeleton geometrically straight but makes the *knee bone's own rotation* deviate far from its bind value — which this model's imperfect auto-rig skin weights render as a visible crease at the knee, even though the joint positions are correct. The fix is to apply the **same** correction quaternion to the thigh and shin (`UpLeg`/`Leg` share one `corr`), rigidly re-aiming the leg as a unit so each bone's rotation stays close to bind. The **foot is deliberately excluded** — giving it the leg's correction tips the toes down ~12° and reads as standing on tiptoe; without it the foot keeps its bind orientation (flat). If a joint ever shows a visible crease despite looking geometrically right, suspect this (check how far the bone's live rotation has drifted from `real.bind[name].q`, not just the joint positions).
  - **Ground snap** (post-pass in `applySkeleton()`): leg FK never lands the feet exactly on the floor (crouch poses sink them, straightened legs float them). After `driveRealBones()`, the lowest of the four foot contact points (`real.ground`: `Left/RightFoot`, `Left/RightToeBase` with their bind-pose heights) is compared to its bind height, `rig.hips.position.y` is shifted by the gap, and `driveRealBones()` re-runs. Sinking is always corrected; floating is only corrected in proportion to `1-.85*walkAmt`, so running keeps a flight phase. This replaced an analytic hip "lift" that over-lifted and made the character look tiptoed — don't hand-tune hip height or the crouch knee-bend formula to fix foot contact.
  - **Locomotion legs come from Meshy's clips** (now the `Walking`/`Running` clips inside `assets/ninja.glb`; before the 2026-10-18 swap: `assets/anim/walk.glb` + `run.glb`, now in the backup folder — skeleton + clip only, in place). `loadLocoClips()` keeps only the 8 leg-bone quaternion tracks of each and self-calibrates them (planted-toe speed: walk ≈1.27 m/s, run ≈6.1 m/s — a sprint; and when the left foot is furthest forward). Both clips run on **one shared normalized phase** `loco.u` (aligned so the left foot is forward at the same u), blended walk→run by speed (`loco.b`), then slerped over the procedural legs by `walkAmt` in `driveRealBones()` — standing/attacking is fully procedural, so slashing mid-run fades the legs out in ~0.3 s. Hips, torso, arms/sword IK stay procedural; `walkPhase` is derived from `loco.u` so arm swing and torso twist stay in step. Tuning knobs at the top of section 3b: `RUN_SPEED` (4.0 m/s) and `WALK_SPEED` (1.6 m/s, while Shift is held — `keys.shift` is refreshed from `e.shiftKey` on every key event; `walkAmt` saturates at `WALK_SPEED` so walking still drives the legs fully), `RUN_LEAN` (extra forward spine lean at full run, head counter-tilts 55% of it); walk↔run blend uses ground speed (walk <2 m/s, run >3.4 m/s) and `CADENCE_BOOST` (1.2 = steps 20% quicker than the stride strictly allows; raise for faster footwork at the cost of some foot slide). Model versions + Meshy task IDs: `assets/raw/README.md`.
  - **Procedural run cycle / toe-in** (now only the fallback, and blended in at low speed): the bind pose turns the toes out ~25°. Most of the inward turn is applied as ankle Y rotation; the thigh gets only a slight knee-in twist, because twisting the thigh far also rotates the knee-bend plane and every bent knee then flicks the shin outward (bow-legged gallop). Spread narrows with `walkAmt`. Thigh swing is asymmetric (further forward than back), the stance knee flexes at mid-stance, and the spine leans forward more while moving.
  - **Running / walking carry (2026-10).** While not attacking, the pose target is `mix(IDLE, CARRY, walkAmt)`: `CARRY` holds the blade upright-forward beside the right hip (tip above the shoulder). The IDLE blade (low, across the front) was inside a swinging leg in ~60% of run frames; now the blade stays ≥18 cm from the leg bones. The sword arm swings only ±4 cm (it used to swing ±15–25 cm, which waved the blade and made the grip solver re-twist the wrist every step), and the run's hip/shoulder counter-twist was halved (hips .06, chest .09 rad). Don't solve "wrist twists while running" by locking the hand to the forearm — tried: the blade then hangs along the leg and the legs pass through it.
  - **Arm/grip continuity (don't regress).** Measured per 4 ms step (hand-vs-forearm °/s, elbow m/s) with `I.stop()` + `frame(0,4,true)`: (1) `gripPass` keeps the elbow swivel continuous — last frame's elbow (`arm.eRel`, committed by the caller) is located on the new circle, moving away costs `GRIP_TUNE.swivelHold`/°, and the result is eased (`tauSwivel`); the pole-based reference elbow itself flips when the shoulder→wrist axis swings past the pole, which made the left elbow jump ~30 cm in one step as it reached for the hilt. The left hang-IK pole is aimed at last frame's elbow while gripping. (2) The blade basis uses the smooth analytic `fall` vector, not `S.edge` (which snaps between fall and the motion direction; since the fist is locked to the blade's roll, every snap spun the wrist at ~40,000°/s). `S.edge` is still used for hit FX. (3) The off-hand grab/release turns the fist from last frame's orientation toward the grip or bind pose (`a.haPrev`), not bind→grip by `blend` (shortest-path slerp flipped when they're ~180° apart; keeping a hemisphere drifted onto a 300° path). (4) The sword is glued to the fist after the arm solve (blade = hand·K⁻¹, G = wrist + palm offset), so the hit test/trail use exactly what's drawn. Results: run/walk/stop wrist ≤~1,000°/s, no elbow jumps; slashes p95 ~2,000°/s (fast cuts), grip held through beats 1–2.
