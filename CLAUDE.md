# CLAUDE.md — Path of the Shinobi: Blood Moon

You are helping build an isometric ink-brush-style action game. The user is new to Blender and 3D, and speaks Thai — converse with them in Thai, explaining steps one at a time and naming exact menu/button labels.

## What the game is
- Protagonist: a ninja in a grey hood with a face covering and black kimono, two swords sheathed on the back, wielding one katana
- Look: black/white/grey ink-brush-on-paper style, with a single spot of red (the torii gate, the final combo kanji)
- 3-beat combo: 一 diagonal downward slash → 二 upward slash → 三 spinning sweep
- Map: a floating isometric island with a stone courtyard, a pond, cuttable bamboo, and straw training dummies

## Client architecture (know this before making changes)
- Units = meters. The character faces +Z; the character's right hand is on the -X side. Height 1.75 m.
- **Color pipeline is pinned to r128 behavior**: `THREE.ColorManagement.enabled=false`, `renderer.outputColorSpace=LinearSRGBColorSpace`, `renderer.useLegacyLights=true` (deprecated in 0.160, removed in r165 — upgrading Three.js past that means retuning light intensities). Don't flip these without comparing screenshots.
- **Two rigs.** The procedural rig (nested `THREE.Group`s in `rig`: `root → hips → spine → chest → neck → head`, legs `thighL/R → kneeL/R → ankleL/R`) is now an **invisible driver**. `applySkeleton()` poses it exactly as before, then `driveRealBones()` copies each driver's rotation delta (relative to its rest) onto the matching real bone of `assets/ninja.glb` (`BONE_MAP`, section `3b`). If the glb fails to load, the procedural body stays visible as a fallback.
  - This model's bones have **no naming prefix** (`Hips`, `Spine`, `LeftArm`, …) and the spine order is reversed vs. a typical Mixamo rig — `Spine02` is closest to the hips, `Spine` (no suffix) is closest to the neck. See `docs/BONES.md`.
  - The Armature node carries a **0.01 scale** (mesh authored in cm-like units). Two consequences: (1) quaternions must be extracted via `Matrix4.extractRotation()` first, never `Quaternion.setFromRotationMatrix()` on a matrix that still has this scale baked in — it silently produces a non-unit quaternion and the whole rig collapses; (2) a `Mesh` parented directly as a child of one of these `Bone` objects doesn't render (looks like a frustum-culling quirk) — world-space props must be positioned every frame via `matrixWorld`, never literally `bone.add(prop)`.
  - Meshy's bind pose has the legs splayed; `real.corr` straightens the legs first so `spread` etc. mean the same as on the driver. **Important:** correcting each leg segment independently (thigh and knee each rotated to point straight down on their own) leaves the skeleton geometrically straight but makes the *knee bone's own rotation* deviate far from its bind value — which this model's imperfect auto-rig skin weights render as a visible crease at the knee, even though the joint positions are correct. The fix is to apply the **same** correction quaternion to the thigh and shin (`UpLeg`/`Leg` share one `corr`), rigidly re-aiming the leg as a unit so each bone's rotation stays close to bind. The **foot is deliberately excluded** — giving it the leg's correction tips the toes down ~12° and reads as standing on tiptoe; without it the foot keeps its bind orientation (flat). If a joint ever shows a visible crease despite looking geometrically right, suspect this (check how far the bone's live rotation has drifted from `real.bind[name].q`, not just the joint positions).
  - **Ground snap** (post-pass in `applySkeleton()`): leg FK never lands the feet exactly on the floor (crouch poses sink them, straightened legs float them). After `driveRealBones()`, the lowest of the four foot contact points (`real.ground`: `Left/RightFoot`, `Left/RightToeBase` with their bind-pose heights) is compared to its bind height, `rig.hips.position.y` is shifted by the gap, and `driveRealBones()` re-runs. Sinking is always corrected; floating is only corrected in proportion to `1-.85*walkAmt`, so running keeps a flight phase. This replaced an analytic hip "lift" that over-lifted and made the character look tiptoed — don't hand-tune hip height or the crouch knee-bend formula to fix foot contact.
  - **Locomotion legs come from Meshy's clips** (`assets/anim/walk.glb` + `run.glb`, the free clips from the rig task — skeleton + clip only, in place). `loadLocoClips()` keeps only the 8 leg-bone quaternion tracks of each and self-calibrates them (planted-toe speed: walk ≈1.27 m/s, run ≈6.1 m/s — a sprint; and when the left foot is furthest forward). Both clips run on **one shared normalized phase** `loco.u` (aligned so the left foot is forward at the same u), blended walk→run by speed (`loco.b`), then slerped over the procedural legs by `walkAmt` in `driveRealBones()` — standing/attacking is fully procedural, so slashing mid-run fades the legs out in ~0.3 s. Hips, torso, arms/sword IK stay procedural; `walkPhase` is derived from `loco.u` so arm swing and torso twist stay in step. Tuning knobs at the top of section 3b: `RUN_SPEED` (4.0 m/s) and `WALK_SPEED` (1.6 m/s, while Shift is held — `keys.shift` is refreshed from `e.shiftKey` on every key event; `walkAmt` saturates at `WALK_SPEED` so walking still drives the legs fully), `RUN_LEAN` (extra forward spine lean at full run, head counter-tilts 55% of it); walk↔run blend uses ground speed (walk <2 m/s, run >3.4 m/s) and `CADENCE_BOOST` (1.2 = steps 20% quicker than the stride strictly allows; raise for faster footwork at the cost of some foot slide). Model versions + Meshy task IDs: `assets/raw/README.md`.
  - **Procedural run cycle / toe-in** (now only the fallback, and blended in at low speed): the bind pose turns the toes out ~25°. Most of the inward turn is applied as ankle Y rotation; the thigh gets only a slight knee-in twist, because twisting the thigh far also rotates the knee-bend plane and every bent knee then flicks the shin outward (bow-legged gallop). Spread narrows with `walkAmt`. Thigh swing is asymmetric (further forward than back), the stance knee flexes at mid-stance, and the spine leans forward more while moving.
  - **Hands / grip.** The hands in `ninja.glb` are now **modelled loose fists** wrapped around a katana hilt (Blender; the Meshy mitten was cut 1.2 cm past the wrist and replaced; ~12.9 cm long, four fingers + thumb with ≥0.5 cm gaps, a 1.9 cm-radius tunnel along (0,0.5,0.866) in hand-local space; the left is the exact x-mirror). There are no finger bones — the fist is a rigid pose baked into the mesh, so the left hand hangs as a closed fist when it's off the sword. Hand-bone local axes (measured; T-pose): fingers +Y, thumb side +Z, palm faces -X on the right hand / +X on the left; palm surface now at x≈∓2.6. `aimHand()` slerps the wrist to `blade * grip.K` each frame (blend 1 for the right hand, `p.two` for the left) and `wristFor()` puts the IK wrist where the palm's grip point (`grip.pt`) lands on the sword's grip point G (= the pose target hx,hy,hz; left hand: G − 0.11·dir). `GRIP_CFG` (palmSign, P = which way the palm faces in blade space, phi = diagonal hammer-grip tilt, pt = a point on the hilt axis inside the tunnel, hand-local cm; currently R `[-3.6,7.8,-0.4]`, L `[3.6,7.8,-0.4]`) builds `real.grip`; it is exposed as `__iso.GRIP_CFG` + `__iso.regrip()` for live tuning. The old identity offset made the blade run along the fingers and out of the wrist (wrist twisted ~155°). **Reach clamp:** the idle target is farther than the arm can reach (0.56 m vs 0.456 m); when the wrist target is out of reach it is pulled back to the reach limit and G follows the hand — without that the sword floats ~10 cm off the fist. **Arm/wrist solving for the grip (don't regress; this took four iterations).** The hammer grip fixes the hand's orientation relative to the blade (fingers 60° off the hilt axis), but the combo keyframes set blade direction and hand position independently, so a fixed-pole IK + fixed grip folded the wrist back (measured: physical wrist bend = angle between the forearm and the hand's finger axis, up to 151° R / 172° L in the slashes; a real wrist manages ~80°), twisted the forearm and snapped the elbow when running started. What the pose function does now (section 4, after the blade basis is built), per frame: (1) `gripPass()` = wrist target G − palm offset, reach clamp (right hand: G follows the hand), IK, then an **elbow swivel search** (±120° around the shoulder→wrist axis, `GRIP_TUNE.swivelBias` prefers the natural side) that picks the elbow putting the forearm closest to the hand's finger axis; (2) `rollToward()` rolls the **blade about its own axis** (ρ, ±`bladeRollMax`=90°, smoothed with `tauRoll`) so the finger axis follows the forearm — only the blade's flat/edge facing changes, tip/trail/hit test don't depend on it; the left hand may roll ±`handRollMax`=90° relative to the right (45° — and a laggy `tauRoll` .07, now .02 — made it let go in most two-handed strike poses); (3) the left hand slides along the hilt (6–17 cm below the right hand, picks the least wrist bend) and **lets go** when even the best spot needs >140° (re-grips below 115°, hysteresis `S.gripOn`). The pose's `two` is read as a boolean (>.5 = grab) and `S.twoEff` eases it over ~150 ms — a fractional grip weight (old: `two:.6` keys, or a partial let-go) blended the hand half-way between the hilt and the hip, so it floated in mid-air; (4) `aimHand` moves the hand's twist about the finger axis (relative to the bind pose `a.q0`, clamped ±`rollMax`=80° — beyond ±90° the sign flips and the forearm would snap) onto the forearm bone, smoothed (`a.rollS`), and `aimArm` re-aims the forearm from `a.foBind` every frame so the twist can't accumulate. Results (wrist bend): idle ≈25°, running mostly <50° (peak ~83° as the run starts), slashes: right ≤~100° in a few frames, left ≤107° while gripping. IDLE blade is now by 30 / el -30 (blade across the body) and both palms use P=[.7071,0,-.7071] (the left fist is the x-mirror; both palms face the same way in a real grip; `palmSign` handles the mirror) — from an analytic scan of (by, el, ψ) for min hinge+0.6·|twist|. How to verify: `I.stop()` then drive `I.press()` + `I.frame(0,16)` (presses at 0/420/860 ms chain the 3 beats), logging per frame the physical wrist bend, `a.rollS`, elbow angle and `S.twoEff`; zoom screenshots at 208/272/336/416/720/1040/1360 ms. `__iso.IDLE` / `GRIP_TUNE` / `GRIP_CFG` + `regrip()` are exposed. **Combo keys were re-authored for this model's reach** (2026-10): the old keys put G 0.55–0.6 m from the right shoulder (authored for the procedural rig) — the arm locked straight, G got reach-clamped, the blade pivoted around a hand that had already arrived, and the left hand let go mid-cut (`twoEff` dipped to 0.2). Keep two-handed keys within ~0.45 m of the right shoulder (chest-local shoulders ≈(∓.19,.17,.02)). Beats 1–2 are four keys (wind-up → mid-swing → strike → follow-through, `hit:2`); beat 3 keeps three. Result: grip held through beats 1–2 (twoEff ≥0.85), clean release mid-spin in beat 3, hits still land on all three beats. Tuning loop used: `__iso.COMBO` is exposed (edit keys live), hold a pose by setting `S.act` to a fake def whose keys are all that pose, hide the world (scene children other than `rig.root`/katana/eyes) and composite crops of the canvas into a contact sheet right after `frame(0,0)` (drawImage works synchronously after render). (An earlier version drew code-built half-ring finger props around the hilt while the hand was still a mitten; they were removed once the fists existed.) The pose result's `wR` is G (blade root for `hitTest`/trail), the IK wrist is `wristR`.

  - **Eyes / face.** The source art's eyes were a flat 2D highlight that Meshy's 3D reconstruction couldn't reproduce (tried `retexture` with a text prompt; it altered unrelated parts of the model and still didn't add eyes — reverted). The face is now a **painted face plate built from `art/reference/face/face.png`** (replaced an earlier sculpted almond-eye strip): the mask opening (eyes, angry brows, nose bridge) was cropped from the reference (ref px x100–516, y296–450), its outline traced from the light skin region, and the crop pasted into an empty strip of `texture_0` (Blender pixels x820–1058, rows 4–92 from the bottom; `normal` there is already flat and metallic/roughness rough 0.8). The plate is a 4-ref-px grid (2,665 verts, 2,515 quads) in `char1`, same material, 100% `Head` weight, vertex group `_eyes`, laid 0.15 cm in front of a polynomial fit of the face surface (fit from BVH ray casts with the old eyes/fists removed; residual <0.1 cm) plus a 0.28 cm nose ridge fading to 0 at the bottom edge so the plate meets the mask cloth flush. On top of that the eye glow (`rig.eyes`, toggled by the `eye` pose parameter) is a code VFX, positioned in world space from `Head.matrixWorld` each frame (see the scale/bone-mesh note above for why it can't just be parented). **`real.eyeLocal` / `real.eyeRot`**: Head-bone-local cm eye centres `(-2.77,10.97,11.35)`, `(3.58,11.59,10.90)` and per-eye `rotateZ` `[-0.053, 0.179]` rad (they differ because the head bone is rolled ~4° at rest; outer end up), glow box 4.4×1.2 cm. The face shows at a low pitch (`CAM.pitchT` ≈ 4–9).
  - **Character material (what was wrong, don't regress it).** The glb has a **normal map** (all the cloth folds live there) and a base-colour texture that is almost black (mean ≈28/255, 96% of pixels <64). The loader replaces the material with a `MeshToonMaterial`; (1) it must carry `normalMap` over — it originally copied only `map`, so every fold vanished and the body read as a flat black silhouette; (2) through the shared 3-band `grad` (darkest band 27%) the dark albedo is still black on the cream floor, so the body uses its **own ramp** `bodyGrad` (118/190/255) + an `onBeforeCompile` albedo lift `0.07+0.93*pow(c,0.42)` + `normalScale` 2.2 (the white rope barely moves, the black trousers/boots get a floor so their folds read). Tuned by eye in the browser; gamma 0.5→0.4 looked too grey. Other scene objects keep the shared `grad`.
  - **Back swords are in the mesh but rigidly weighted.** Meshy skinned them mostly to the thigh bones (`LeftUpLeg`/`RightUpLeg` 30–80%), so they bent/swung with the legs when walking or running. In the glb their ~3,900 vertices are now 100% `Spine01` (picked in Blender: thin islands aligned with the sword axis ≈(0.57,0.08,-0.82), plus seam-duplicate vertices coincident with them, plus tiny islands touching them). The Meshy mesh has the swords shredded into hundreds of islands, so the hilt tops still read as a fan of thin strands. Cloak pieces overlapping the swords are deliberately left on the leg/spine weights.
- **Arms are not driven by the driver rig.** 2-bone IK (`solveIK`) runs from the real `LeftArm/RightArm` positions with the real segment lengths, then `aimArm()` aims the real upper/fore-arm bones at the IK elbow/wrist (with a twist so the mesh's elbow hinges in the bend plane). The right arm holds the sword (placed at the IK wrist); the left arm hangs or grips the hilt (`two`).
- **Poses are parameters, not direct bone keyframes**: `IDLE = {hx,hy,hz (hand position in chest-local coordinates), by,el (blade direction), hipY, hipYaw, chestYaw, lean, headYaw, spin, spread, two, eye, glint}`
- `COMBO[]` = a list of timelines `keys:[[time ms, pose, ease], …]` (any number of keys; eases `out`/`in`/`lin`/`snap`/`io`) plus `hit` (index of the strike key, default 1 — the lunge and trail run keys[0]→keys[hit]; the hit test runs keys[0]→last key+40), `impactMin`, `chain` (the time window to chain into the next combo hit), `end`
- Every parameter passes through `SOD` (second-order dynamics) before use; the (f, ζ, r) values differ per group (hand/blade/body/spin). This is what keeps poses from looking mechanical — **do not replace it with plain lerp**.
- The "hit" beat is detected from the sword tip's velocity starting to decrease, not from a fixed time (`maxSp`, `decel`). That's what triggers hit-stop, camera shake, and the combo kanji.
- Hit detection uses the distance from the blade's line segment (wrist→tip) to the target's axis on the XZ plane (`hitTest`)
- The loop uses a fixed 4ms substep (`step(T)`). The camera is orthographic isometric (yaw 45°, pitch 35.26°), rotating 90° at a time with Q/E.
- Debug: `window.__iso` exposes `{S, step, frame, stop, press, CAM, rotateCam, tiltCam, rig, GRIP_CFG, GRIP_TUNE, IDLE, COMBO, regrip}`, used to drive the game deterministically for headless-browser testing (`stop()` then `frame(0,16)` repeatedly to step; `rig.real` holds the loaded model/bones)
  - Console tips: `THREE` is **not** a global (ES module) — borrow constructors from live objects (`real.hipsPos.constructor` is `Vector3`). `S.facing` (rad; ≈`Math.PI*.25` turns the character toward the default camera) and `S.pos` can be set directly; `CAM.zoomT` smaller = **closer** (min ≈0.55), `CAM.pitchT` in degrees; `stop()` freezes the loop for good (reload to resume). To see the face, set a low pitch — see the Eyes note.
  - **Browser caches `ninja.glb`**: `server/main.go` serves `../assets` with `http.FileServer` and no `Cache-Control`, so after replacing the glb a plain reload keeps showing the OLD model (checking `geometry.attributes.position.count` is the quick tell: 30,771 = original Meshy mesh, 31,860 = with the sculpted eyes, 35,770 = with the old sculpted eyes + fists, 37,346 = current with the painted face plate + fists). Hard-reload (Cmd+Shift+R), or in automation `await fetch('/assets/ninja.glb',{cache:'reload'})` then `location.reload()`.

### Pitfalls when editing `ninja.glb` in Blender (all hit in practice)
- **Restart `claude` after approving the MCP server** (`claude --continue` keeps the conversation) — the `mcp__blender__*` tools don't appear in a session that started before approval, even though `claude mcp list` shows ✔ Connected.
- **glTF import oddities**: the Armature is scaled 0.01 and `char1` vertex coordinates are in cm (world = local × 0.01); the bones are drawn as huge octahedra and `Icosphere` (collection `glTF_not_exported`) is the importer's bone shape — don't delete it. The huge bone shapes + Icosphere swallow the `look` camera (grey/blank screenshots): hide `Armature` and `Icosphere` before `look`, and to frame the head use an Empty at the head as `target` with a small `distance`. `get_scene_info` lists only top-level objects, so `char1` (child of Armature) looks "missing" — it isn't.
- **Check `char1`'s rotation before exporting.** A fresh import has `rotation_quaternion = (1,0,0,0)`; an accidental drag/rotate in the viewport (it happened: ~7° about X) silently tilts the exported mesh. Compare the exported `POSITION` accessor with the known-good bounds `min [-0.813, 0, -0.279]`, `max [0.813, 1.75, 0.279]`; also `Armature` and `char1` `matrix_world` must be pure 0.01 scale. Don't leave Blender in Edit Armature mode either.
- **The imported Armature carries an action** (`Armature|clip0|baselayer`) that re-evaluates the pose on frame changes — reset pose-bone quaternions/locations (and return to Object mode) before exporting; pose a test, then reset.
- **Export recipe** that round-trips cleanly (identical node/bone structure, 1 material, 3 PNG images, `TANGENT` kept): select `Armature` + `char1`, `bpy.ops.export_scene.gltf(filepath=…, export_format='GLB', use_selection=True, export_skins=True, export_animations=False, export_tangents=True)`. The baked rest clip is dropped on purpose (the game only reads animations from `assets/anim/walk.glb` and `run.glb`). Verify with `python3 tools/inspect_glb.py`. Keep a copy of the previous glb (it's also in git LFS) — `git checkout assets/ninja.glb` restores it.
- **Adding geometry to `char1`** (the eyes): append to the same mesh with the same material slot, weight 100% to a bone via the deform layer, and give it **non-degenerate UVs** — if every vertex of a part shares one UV the exporter writes a zero `TANGENT`, and three.js turns the normal-mapped surface black (NaN): the first eye plates were invisible in-game for exactly this reason while looking fine in Blender. Anything you paint into the atlas corner must also be neutral in `normal` (black decodes to a garbage normal) and `metallic_roughness`. Verify that edited texture pixels survive the export by extracting the PNGs from the exported glb. Don't add a second mesh/material (see the `skinned` note in follow-ups). The face front is at world y≈-0.19 (mesh-local ≈-18.9 cm) and strongly curved (drops ~2.7 cm between x=0 and x=±5); the hood brim's lower edge arches over it at z≈164 cm.
- **Vertex order / groups**: char1 is now `0–29073` original non-face/non-fist mesh (29,074 verts), then the fists (`_fists`, 3,566), then the painted face plate (`_eyes`, 2,665), 35,305 total in Blender (37,346 in the glb after UV-seam splits). Indices shift whenever a part is rebuilt — always select by the `_eyes` / `_fists` vertex groups (they are not bones, so the exporter ignores them for skinning). The fists use a 32×32 glove tile at pixel (108,80) of `texture_0` (value 22/255; a black tile lifts to ~0.07 in the game, the same as the outline, so fingers wouldn't read) — a Meshy re-export or texture repack loses it and the face strip. The old eye atlas tiles in the bottom-left corner are now unused. Rollback sources (unsaved .blend, fake-user datablocks): mesh `mesh_before_face` + image `texture_0_before_face` (before the face plate), mesh `orig_mesh_keep` (original Meshy), or git.
- **Re-weighting by connected island**: vertex-group `remove()` while iterating `v.groups` leaves the old weights in place; use a bmesh deform layer (`v[dl].clear(); v[dl][idx]=1.0`). Meshy meshes are split at UV seams, so coincident duplicate vertices exist — weight them together or the seam tears.
